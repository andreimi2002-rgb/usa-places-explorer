### History

The Kia Center opened on October 1, 2010, at 400 West Church Street in downtown Orlando, replacing the Orlando Arena, the Magic's first home, which had opened in 1989 and hosted the 1995 Finals. The new building is owned and run by the City of Orlando through its Venues department, which also operates Camping World Stadium.

For its first thirteen years the arena carried the name of Amway, the company co-founded by the late Magic owner Rich DeVos. In December 2023 a naming deal with the carmaker Kia gave it its current name. Orange County has approved $226 million over ten years for upgrades, paid from its tourist tax on hotel stays.

### More than basketball

Like most American arenas, it is busy far beyond the NBA season. In its first fifteen years it staged 3,291 events seen by nearly 20 million guests, among them:

- 644 Magic games and 494 games of the Orlando Solar Bears, the city's minor-league ice hockey team;
- 496 concerts, 99 Disney on Ice shows, 26 WWE wrestling events and 4 UFC nights;
- 7 NHL pre-season games, 3 NCAA men's tournament games and an NBA All-Star Weekend;
- 144 high-school graduations.

The Magic play 41 regular-season home games a year here, usually between late October and mid-April, plus any playoff games.

### Getting there

The arena is downtown, just steps from the Church Street station of SunRail, the commuter train that runs north–south through the Orlando area. Under the "Train to the Game" programme, Magic ticket holders can ride SunRail free to and from weekday home games by showing their ticket.
