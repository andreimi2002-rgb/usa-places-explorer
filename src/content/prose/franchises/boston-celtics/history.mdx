### Founding and Auerbach (1946–1956)

The Celtics played their first season in 1946–47. In 1950 owner Walter Brown hired Red Auerbach as coach. Auerbach would stay with the club for 57 years, as coach, general manager and president.

### The Russell dynasty (1956–1969)

In 1956 Boston gave the St. Louis Hawks Ed Macauley and rookie Cliff Hagan, and St. Louis used the No. 2 pick of the <Term id="nba-draft">draft</Term> on Bill Russell for Boston. With Russell at center, the Celtics won their first title in 1957 and then eight in a row from 1959 to 1966. Russell coached and played for the 1968 and 1969 champions, then retired.

### Havlicek, Cowens and the 1970s (1969–1979)

John Havlicek, Dave Cowens and Jo Jo White led Boston to two more titles: in 1974 against Kareem Abdul-Jabbar's Milwaukee Bucks, and in 1976 against Phoenix.

### Larry Bird (1979–1992)

Boston drafted Larry Bird sixth overall in 1978, and he joined the team for the 1979–80 season, when he was named Rookie of the Year. The Bird Celtics won titles in 1981, 1984 and 1986, and he was the league's <Term id="mvp">MVP</Term> three years running, from 1984 to 1986.

### The new Big Three (2008)

Paul Pierce, drafted by Boston in 1998, was joined by Kevin Garnett and Ray Allen, and the three won the 2008 title against the Lakers, with Pierce named <Term id="finals-mvp">Finals MVP</Term>.

### Banner 18 (2013–today)

Led by Jayson Tatum and Jaylen Brown, and coached by Joe Mazzulla, Boston beat Dallas in five games in 2024 for its 18th title, moving one ahead of the Lakers. In 2025 the NBA approved the sale of the club to a group led by Bill Chisholm.

In July 2026 Boston traded Jaylen Brown to Philadelphia for Paul George, leaving Jayson Tatum as the team's main star.
