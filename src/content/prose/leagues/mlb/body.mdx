---
title: How MLB works
---

Major League Baseball has 30 teams, 29 in the United States and one in Canada, the Toronto Blue Jays. They are split into two leagues, the American League and the National League, each with three divisions. The National League dates from 1876 and the American League from 1901; since 1903 their champions have met in the <Term id="world-series">World Series</Term>.

The regular season is long: 162 games from late March to September, almost every day, usually in series of three or four games against the same opponent. Twelve teams then play the <Term id="playoffs">playoffs</Term> in October. A game lasts nine <Term id="inning">innings</Term> with no clock and no draws; the duel between the <Term id="pitcher">pitcher</Term> and the batter is at its heart, and the <Term id="home-run">home run</Term> is its most famous play.

There is no salary cap in baseball, so rich clubs can spend far more than others, but they pay a tax above a set level. This encyclopedia counts World Series titles, from 1903 to 2026; the New York Yankees have won the most, 27.
