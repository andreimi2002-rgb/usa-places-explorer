title: History by eras
---

### Beginnings (1986–1992)

Orlando's NBA story starts with two men. Pat Williams, then the general manager of the Philadelphia 76ers, and the local businessman Jimmy Hewitt launched a bid in 1986, and on April 22, 1987, the NBA awarded the city a franchise. It was part of a wave of growth: Charlotte and Miami began play in 1988, and Orlando and Minnesota followed a year later.

The first regular-season game, on November 4, 1989, was a five-point loss to the New Jersey Nets at the brand-new Orlando Arena. That first season ended 18–64, the usual reality for an <Term id="expansion-team">expansion team</Term> built from other clubs' spare players. Its first ever draft pick, the shooting guard Nick Anderson, would stay for a decade. In September 1991 the DeVos family bought the club. The first three seasons under coach Matt Guokas brought 18, 31 and 21 wins.

### The first peak (1992–1996)

Everything changed in 1992. Teams with poor records get the best odds in the <Term id="draft-lottery">draft lottery</Term>, and Orlando came away with the first pick. It chose Shaquille O'Neal, a center so big and agile that he was named Rookie of the Year straight away, and the team jumped from 21 to 41 wins. A year later the club pulled off a bold draft-night <Term id="trade">trade</Term>: it picked Chris Webber first overall, then swapped him to Golden State for Penny Hardaway and three future first-round picks.

Under coach Brian Hill the team won 50 games in 1993-94 and 57 in 1994-95. That spring Orlando beat Boston, then Michael Jordan's Chicago Bulls (4–2) and Indiana in seven games, and reached the NBA Finals in only its sixth season. The Houston Rockets swept them 4–0. In 1995-96 the Magic won a club-record 60 games and swept Detroit, beat Atlanta, but were swept in the conference finals by a Bulls team that won 72 games that year. In July 1996 O'Neal signed with the Los Angeles Lakers as a <Term id="free-agency">free agent</Term>, and the window closed.

### Searching for a second act (1996–2004)

Hardaway became the face of the team, and in the 1997 <Term id="playoffs">playoffs</Term> he scored 42 and 41 points in back-to-back games against Miami, though Orlando still lost the series. The Hall of Fame coach Chuck Daly led the team for two seasons, and in 1999 Doc Rivers took over and was named Coach of the Year in his first season, after a 41–41 year with a roster of little-known players.

In 2000 the Magic signed Tracy McGrady and Grant Hill. Hill's ankle injuries kept him off the court for most of his seven seasons, but McGrady became one of the best scorers in the world, leading the league in 2002–03 and 2003–04. The team reached the playoffs three years in a row and lost in the first round each time; in 2003-04 it fell to 21–61 and Rivers was dismissed after a 1–10 start.

### The Dwight Howard years (2004–2012)

That bad season brought another first overall pick: Dwight Howard, who arrived in the same 2004 draft as the point guard Jameer Nelson. Howard grew into the league's dominant defender and rebounder, winning Defensive Player of the Year three seasons running from 2008–09. Under Stan Van Gundy, hired in 2007, and with Hedo Türkoğlu and Rashard Lewis as shooting forwards around him, Orlando won 52, 59 and 59 games.

In 2009 the Magic beat Philadelphia, the reigning champions Boston in seven games and LeBron James's Cleveland, and returned to the Finals, where the Lakers won 4–1. In 2010 they swept Charlotte and Atlanta before losing the conference finals to Boston; it remains the last time Orlando won a playoff series. A new downtown arena opened that autumn. In August 2012 Howard was traded to the Lakers in a four-team deal that brought back six players, among them a young Nikola Vučević, and five draft picks.

### The long rebuild (2012–2021)

The years after Howard were the hardest in the club's history: six seasons in a row without the playoffs and four different coaches. Vučević grew into a two-time All-Star and the club's second-best rebounder of all time, and Aaron Gordon, the fourth pick of 2014, grew up alongside him. Under Steve Clifford the Magic reached the playoffs in 2019 and 2020, then traded Vučević and Gordon in March 2021 and started again.

### A new core (2021–today)

The rebuild was built through the draft: Jalen Suggs and Franz Wagner in 2021, then Paolo Banchero with the first overall pick in 2022, who was named Rookie of the Year the following spring. Under coach Jamahl Mosley the team went from 22 wins in 2021-22 to division titles in 2023-24 and 2024-25, but each playoff run ended in the first round, the last of them a seven-game loss to Detroit in 2026. In June 2026 the club hired Sean Sweeney, an associate head coach from San Antonio, as the 16th head coach in its history, and brought Vučević back for a second spell.
