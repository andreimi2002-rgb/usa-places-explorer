### Name, colours and mascot

The team was named before it existed. In July 1986, while Orlando was still bidding for a franchise, a public name-the-team contest produced four finalists (Heat, Tropics, Juice and Magic) and "Magic" won, a nod to the theme parks that define the region. The club's colours are blue, black and silver, and its mascot since the first season, in 1989, has been Stuff the Magic Dragon.

### Ownership

The DeVos family, co-founders of the Amway company, bought the Magic in September 1991 and still own them; the family has run the club for more than three decades.

### How the current team is built

Today's Magic are a team grown mostly through the <Term id="nba-draft">draft</Term>. Paolo Banchero (first overall in 2022), Franz Wagner (eighth in 2021) and Jalen Suggs (fifth in 2021) were all Orlando picks. Around them the club added the shooting guard Desmond Bane in 2025 and, in 2026, brought back the veteran center Nikola Vučević, who had spent most of a decade in Orlando before moving to Chicago in 2021.

The identity is defence first. Orlando had the league's second-best defence in 2024-25, slipping to 13th in 2025-26, while its offence has been below the league average for 14 seasons in a row. The Magic have reached the <Term id="playoffs">playoffs</Term> in each of the last three seasons but lost in the first round every time, and they have not won a playoff series since 2010. Sean Sweeney, hired in June 2026, is in his first season as a head coach.

The Magic did not retire a player's number until their 35th season, when Shaquille O'Neal's No. 32 was raised to the arena roof on February 13, 2024. The club also keeps its own Hall of Fame, with 13 members by 2025: Pat Williams, Nick Anderson, Shaquille O'Neal, Rich DeVos, Penny Hardaway, Jimmy Hewitt, Tracy McGrady, the broadcaster David Steele, Darrell Armstrong, the former general manager John Gabriel, the coach Brian Hill, Dennis Scott and Dwight Howard.

The most successful coach in the playoffs is Stan Van Gundy, who went 31–28 there between 2007 and 2012 and took the team to the 2009 Finals; Brian Hill, over two spells, won the most regular-season games (267).
