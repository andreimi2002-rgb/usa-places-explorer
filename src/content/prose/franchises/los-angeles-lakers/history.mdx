title: History by eras
---

### Minneapolis (1947–1960)

The Lakers' first season was 1947–48, in the National Basketball League. A year later they moved to the Basketball Association of America, the forerunner of the NBA, and won its title in 1949. With the center George Mikan they then won the NBA title in 1950, 1952, 1953 and 1954.

### West, Baylor and the move (1960–1975)

Before the 1960–61 season the club became the NBA's first West Coast team. In Los Angeles, Elgin Baylor and the newly drafted Jerry West made the Lakers a fixture in the Finals, but they kept losing there, six times to Boston in the 1960s alone. The breakthrough came in 1971–72: a 33-game winning streak and the team's first title in Los Angeles.

### Showtime (1975–1991)

In 1975 the Lakers traded for Kareem Abdul-Jabbar, and in 1979 Jerry Buss bought the team and the Lakers drafted Magic Johnson first overall. Pat Riley, a former Lakers guard, became head coach in November 1981. With Johnson running the fast-paced style known as Showtime, the Lakers won five titles in the 1980s: 1980, 1982, 1985, 1987 and 1988. The 1988 team was the first to repeat as champion since Boston in 1969.

### Shaq and Kobe (1996–2004)

In 1996 the Lakers signed Shaquille O'Neal away from Orlando, and an 18-year-old Kobe Bryant began his rookie season. Together they won a <Term id="three-peat">three-peat</Term>, in 2000, 2001 and 2002, with O'Neal named <Term id="finals-mvp">Finals MVP</Term> each time.

### Kobe's second act (2004–2016)

After a 2008 <Term id="trade">trade</Term> for Pau Gasol, Bryant led the Lakers to titles in 2009 and 2010, the second against Boston. He played all twenty seasons of his career with the club and scored 60 points in his final game in 2016.

### LeBron and after (2018–today)

LeBron James joined as a <Term id="free-agency">free agent</Term> in July 2018, and the Lakers traded for Anthony Davis a year later. On October 11, 2020, they beat Miami to win their 17th title. In 2025 the NBA approved the sale of majority control of the club from the Buss family to Mark Walter.

LeBron James left for Philadelphia in the summer of 2026, and Luka Dončić, who joined the Lakers in 2025, became the team's leader.
