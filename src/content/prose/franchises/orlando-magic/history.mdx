title: History by eras
---

### Beginnings (1987–1992)

The NBA gave Orlando a franchise on April 22, 1987, after co-founders Pat Williams and Jimmy Hewitt made the city's case. It was part of a wave of growth: Charlotte and Miami began play in 1988, and Orlando and Minnesota followed a year later.

The first regular-season game, on November 4, 1989, was a five-point loss to the New Jersey Nets at the brand-new Orlando Arena. That first season ended 18–64, the usual reality for a team built from other clubs' spare players. Its first ever draft pick, Nick Anderson, would become a fixture of the team for a decade. In September 1991 the DeVos family bought the club, and it still owns it today.

### The first peak (1992–1996)

Everything changed in 1992. Teams with poor records get the best odds in the <Term id="draft-lottery">draft lottery</Term>, and Orlando came away with the first pick. It chose Shaquille O'Neal, a center so big and agile that he was named Rookie of the Year straight away. A year later the club pulled off a bold draft-night <Term id="trade">trade</Term>: it picked Chris Webber first overall, then swapped him to Golden State for Penny Hardaway and three future first-round picks.

With O'Neal inside and Hardaway running the game, Orlando reached the NBA Finals in 1995, its sixth season. The Houston Rockets swept them 4–0, but the young team looked built to come back. Instead, in July 1996 O'Neal signed with the Los Angeles Lakers as a <Term id="free-agency">free agent</Term>, and the window closed.

### Searching for a second act (1996–2004)

Hardaway became the face of the team, and in the 1997 <Term id="playoffs">playoffs</Term> he scored 42 and 41 points in back-to-back games against Miami, though Orlando still lost the series. In 2000 the Magic added Tracy McGrady, who led the whole league in scoring in 2002–03 and 2003–04.

### The Dwight Howard years (2004–2012)

Another first overall pick, Dwight Howard, arrived in 2004 and grew into the league's dominant defender, winning Defensive Player of the Year three seasons running from 2008–09. In 2009 he led Orlando back to the Finals, where the Lakers won 4–1. A new downtown arena opened in 2010, and in August 2012 Howard was traded to the Lakers in a four-team deal that brought back six players and five draft picks.

### Rebuilding and the present (2012–today)

The years after Howard were a long rebuild. In 2022 Orlando again picked first and chose Paolo Banchero, who was named Rookie of the Year the following spring. After a 45–37 season that ended in the first round, the club hired, in June 2026, Sean Sweeney, an associate head coach from San Antonio, as the 16th head coach in its history.
