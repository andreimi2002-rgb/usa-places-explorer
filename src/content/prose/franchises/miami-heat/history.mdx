title: History by eras
---

### Beginnings (1988–1995)

The Heat played their first season in 1988–89, the same year as the Charlotte Hornets, as part of the same wave of NBA growth that brought Orlando and Minnesota a year later.

### The Riley foundation (1995–2003)

In September 1995 Miami sent a first-round pick and money to New York for the rights to Pat Riley, and named him head coach and team president the next day. That year Micky Arison, the founder's son, also took control of the franchise. Alonzo Mourning arrived the same season and became the team's first star; he was twice named Defensive Player of the Year.

### Wade, Shaq and the first title (2003–2010)

Miami drafted Dwyane Wade fifth overall in 2003, and in 2004 Shaquille O'Neal joined from the Lakers. In 2006, with Riley back on the bench, the Heat lost the first two games of the Finals against Dallas and then won the next four. Wade was named <Term id="finals-mvp">Finals MVP</Term>, and Mourning, back for a second spell, won his only title.

### The Big Three (2010–2014)

In the summer of 2010 the Heat re-signed Wade and brought in LeBron James and Chris Bosh through <Term id="sign-and-trade">sign-and-trade</Term> deals. The three reached the Finals in each of their four seasons together. They lost to Dallas in 2011, then beat Oklahoma City in 2012 and San Antonio, in seven games, in 2013. James was Finals MVP both times. San Antonio won the rematch in 2014, and James left that summer.

### After the Big Three (2014–today)

The Heat kept returning to the top. They reached the Finals in 2020, when Jimmy Butler scored a 40-point triple-double in Game 3 but the Lakers won the series, and again in 2023, losing to Denver. Spoelstra remains head coach, Riley remains president, and Wade's place in the club's story is marked with a statue outside the arena.

In July 2026 the Heat traded for Giannis Antetokounmpo, the two-time MVP, who joins Bam Adebayo at the center of the 2026–27 team.
