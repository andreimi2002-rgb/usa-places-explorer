### Beginnings (1966–1984)

The Bulls joined the NBA for the 1966–67 season. Jerry Sloan, a guard on that first team, later coached the Bulls.

### Jordan arrives (1984–1989)

With the third pick of the 1984 <Term id="nba-draft">draft</Term>, Chicago took Michael Jordan, a 6-foot-6 guard from North Carolina. In 1985 a group led by Jerry Reinsdorf bought control of the team, and in 1987 the Bulls traded for Scottie Pippen, whom Seattle had picked fifth in that year's draft.

### The first three-peat (1989–1993)

Phil Jackson replaced Doug Collins as head coach for the 1989–90 season. The Bulls won the title in 1991, 1992 and 1993, a <Term id="three-peat">three-peat</Term>, with Jordan named <Term id="finals-mvp">Finals MVP</Term> each time. Jordan then retired and tried professional baseball.

### The second three-peat (1995–1998)

Jordan returned during the 1994–95 season, and the Bulls, now playing at the new United Center, built a second dynasty. Their 1995–96 team finished 72–10, a record at the time, and the Bulls won three more titles in 1996, 1997 and 1998.

### After Jordan (1998–today)

The championship team broke up after 1998. For 2026–27 the Bulls have a new head coach, Tiago Splitter, and a young core around Josh Giddey, Matas Buzelis and Caleb Wilson, the fourth pick of the 2026 draft.
