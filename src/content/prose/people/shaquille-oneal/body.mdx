### Orlando (1992–1996)

Shaquille O'Neal, from Newark, New Jersey, played college basketball at LSU and entered the 1992 <Term id="nba-draft">draft</Term> as the most anticipated prospect in years. Orlando, three seasons old, won the draft lottery and took him first overall. At 7 feet 1 inch (2.16 m) and well over 130 kg he was bigger than almost everyone, yet quick enough to run the floor, and he was named Rookie of the Year. The team went from 21 wins to 41 in his first season.

With Penny Hardaway beside him from 1993, Orlando became a contender. In 1995, the franchise's sixth season, O'Neal led it past Boston, Michael Jordan's Bulls and Indiana to the NBA Finals, where Houston won in four games. A year later the Magic won 60 games, but in July 1996 O'Neal left for the Los Angeles Lakers as a <Term id="free-agency">free agent</Term>.

### After Orlando

In Los Angeles, with Kobe Bryant, he won three titles in a row (2000, 2001 and 2002), was <Term id="finals-mvp">Finals MVP</Term> each time and was named the league's <Term id="mvp">MVP</Term> for 1999-2000. Traded to Miami in 2004, he won a fourth title there in 2006 beside Dwyane Wade. He finished his career with Phoenix, Cleveland and Boston and retired in 2011.

### Legacy

O'Neal was a 15-time <Term id="all-star">All-Star</Term> and was named to 14 <Term id="all-nba">All-NBA</Term> teams. He entered the Magic's Hall of Fame in 2015 and the Naismith <Term id="hall-of-fame">Hall of Fame</Term> in 2016. On February 13, 2024, his No. 32 became the first number ever retired by the Magic.
