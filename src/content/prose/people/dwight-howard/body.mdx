### The No. 1 pick

Dwight Howard, from Atlanta, went straight from high school to the 2004 <Term id="nba-draft">draft</Term>, where Orlando, after a 21–61 season, took him first overall. A powerful, athletic center, he became an <Term id="all-star">All-Star</Term> in his third season.

### The best defender in the league (2004–2012)

Under Stan Van Gundy, Orlando was built around him: Howard inside, shooters such as Hedo Türkoğlu and Rashard Lewis outside. He led the league in rebounding five times and in blocks twice, and was named Defensive Player of the Year three seasons in a row, from 2008-09 to 2010-11. In 2009 he carried the Magic past Philadelphia, the defending champions Boston and LeBron James's Cleveland to the NBA Finals, lost 4–1 to the Lakers. A year later Orlando reached the conference finals again.

In August 2012 he was traded to the Lakers in a four-team deal that brought Orlando six players and five draft picks.

### After Orlando

Howard played for the Lakers, Houston, Atlanta, Charlotte, Washington and Philadelphia, and won his only title in 2020 in a second spell with the Lakers.

### Legacy

He is the Magic's career leader in points (11,435), rebounds (8,072) and blocks (1,344). He was an eight-time All-Star and an eight-time <Term id="all-nba">All-NBA</Term> pick, and in 2025 entered both the Naismith <Term id="hall-of-fame">Hall of Fame</Term> and the Magic's own.
