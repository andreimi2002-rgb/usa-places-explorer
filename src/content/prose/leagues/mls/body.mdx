---
title: How MLS works
---

Major League Soccer began in 1996 with ten clubs, a legacy of the 1994 World Cup held in the United States. It has grown to 30 clubs, 27 in the United States and three in Canada, split into an Eastern and a Western Conference of 15.

Four things work differently from a European league. The season follows the calendar year, from late February to early December. The club with the best regular season wins the <Term id="supporters-shield">Supporters' Shield</Term>, but the champion is decided in <Term id="playoffs">playoffs</Term> ending with the <Term id="mls-cup">MLS Cup</Term> final. There is no <Term id="promotion-and-relegation">promotion or relegation</Term>: new clubs join as <Term id="expansion-team">expansion teams</Term>. And spending is controlled by a <Term id="salary-cap">salary cap</Term>, with up to three <Term id="designated-player">Designated Players</Term> per club allowed outside it.

The LA Galaxy have won the most MLS Cups, six. The 2026 regular season ends on November 7 and the MLS Cup final is set for December 18.
